 · step 2 - snapping 
   |    MAAI
This pipeline step snaps sensors (parking and pedestrian) and aggregates CLUE features to the arterial street geometries. This operation helps to 
deﬁne the characteristics of the street by understanding what are the functions in each street.

===== PAGE 20 =====
 · step 3 - temporal features
   |    MAAI
For every 15-minute slot across the study period it records what time of day it is, what day of the week, whether it's a holiday, and what the 
weather was like.

===== PAGE 21 =====
 · step 4 - building graph
   |    MAAI
It builds two relationship maps of Melbourne streets. One is connecting streets that physically meet at intersections, and another one is connecting 
streets that are functionally similar (same mix of cafes, jobs, activity)  even if geographically distant.
spatial graph: two streets are connected if their geometries physically 
touch (share a boundary point), with edge weight decaying by centroid 
distance via a Gaussian kernel.
semantic graph: streets are embedded as feature vectors (jobs, 
cafes, etc.), cosine similarity is computed across all pairs, and only 
mutual top-K neighbours above a similarity threshold are kept as 
edges.

===== PAGE 22 =====
 · step 5.1 - parking reconstruction 
   |    MAAI
it reconstructs parking occupancy from raw sensor events.
Parking sensors only log when a car arrives or leaves, so 
parking reconstruction operation replays that event history to 
derive occupancy at every 15-minute slot. Each sensor was 
snapped to the streets they are belong to. This operation 
creates occupancy rate by looking at the occupancy of each 
sensor in 15min time bin.

===== PAGE 23 =====
 · step 5.2 - pedestrian imputation
   |    MAAI
an XGBoost model was trained on the streets that do have sensors, learning the relationship between a street's static proﬁle (cafes, jobs, etc.), the 
time of day, and its observed pedestrian count. That trained model is then applied to the sensor-less streets to predict what their counts would likely 
be.
