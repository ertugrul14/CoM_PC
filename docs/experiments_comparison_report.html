Experiments & Training Versions

Curbside Intensification — detailed comparison of every trained model and what each proves  ·  2026-06-04

Four models were trained across two cube generations. Two are full deployable models (they differ only by the data cube they were trained on); two are controlled ablation conditions (they differ by which graph and which loss scope is used). This document reports each version’s exact configuration and results, then decomposes why the results differ.

Read the two MAE columns separately All-street MAE averages over all 1,397 modelled streets; sensor MAE averages over only the 74 streets with real pedestrian counters. The sensor streets are the busiest and most variable in the city, so their absolute error is much larger. The two columns measure different populations — compare down each column, never across.

What it proves Keep the full graph for connectivity, but compute the pedestrian loss only on real sensors. This gives the best sensor accuracy (24.97) — better than the deployed model (28.0) — because it never trains on the noisy imputed targets (median imputation R² ≈ 0.18). The trade-off: it is mediocre on all-street coverage (7.5 vs the deployed 4.45), which is exactly why it is an experiment, not the product.

4 · Why the results differ — two independent levers

The three new-cube models isolate two separate effects. They must not be merged.

LeverComparisonEffect on sensor MAEMechanism
Network connectivity V2 (74-node) → V3 (full graph) 54.71 → 24.97
(−30, ≈ 2.2×)		 The unsensored streets are the bridges between sparse sensors. Including them restores message-passing; removing them isolates 35/74 nodes.
Loss masking V1 (unmasked) → V3 (masked), both full graph 28.02 → 24.97
(−3)		 Not training on imputed (noisy) pedestrian targets stops the model from learning to reproduce guesses, sharpening the real-sensor fit.
Do not confuse the levers Connectivity comes from including the full graph, not from the mask. The unmasked deployed model (V1) also has the full graph — which is why it sits at 28, not 55. The mask adds a separate, smaller gain on top.

6 · Comparability & honesty notes

Critical
  • Old vs new ablation are not comparable. An earlier single-seed ablation on the old cube reported ExpA ≈ ExpB ≈ 22 with linear de-normalisation. That used a different dataset and a different inverse transform; the new-cube ablation (this report) reverses the conclusion — connectivity now clearly helps. Only the within-this-run ranking (V1/V2/V3) is valid; one cannot claim “ExpA went 22 → 55”.
  • Direction is certain; magnitude is scale-sensitive. The expm1 inverse of the log1p target amplifies errors on high-count streets, so the precise 2.2× ratio is softer than it looks. Across all 3 seeds the ranking never changes: full graph ≫ sensor-only.
  • Sensor MAE ≠ all-street MAE. 4.45 (V1 all-street) and 25–55 (sensor) describe different populations. Never present them as a regression of each other.

7 · Bottom line

  • Best sensor accuracy: V3 (ExpB), 24.97 ± 0.28 — full graph + masked loss.
  • Best coverage / deployed product: V1, all-street MAE 4.45.
  • Worst: V2 (ExpA), 54.71 — proves the in-between streets are essential, not optional.
  • Thesis claim, substantiated with error bars: imputed network connectivity materially improves pedestrian-flow accuracy on the streets we can verify; the unsensored streets carry the message-passing that links the sparse sensors.
