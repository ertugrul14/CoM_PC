From Ablation to a Foundation-Fixed Model

Methodology evolution of the Curbside Intensification spatio-temporal GNN · Dual-head MultiGCN, 1,397 streets × 14,400 bins · documented 4 June 2026.

This note records two distinct pieces of work and how they relate: (1) an ablation study (Experiments A and B) that probed how the model should be trained and evaluated, and (2) a later round of data-foundation fixes whose retrained model improved on the previous production model. It also states plainly which numbers are comparable and which are not, so the record cannot be misread.

1. Background — the two prediction targets and what is "real"

The graph has 1,397 streets. Only a minority carry sensors; the rest are estimated:

SignalReal (measured)Estimated / filled
Pedestrian flow74 streets1,323 (XGBoost-imputed)
Parking occupancy143 streets1,254 (filled = "unknown")

This split is the reason evaluation must be reported carefully: an average over all 1,397 streets is dominated by the easy, model-generated ones, whereas an average over only the sensored streets reflects genuine predictive skill. The two numbers differ by roughly 5×.

What Phase 1 established

  • The headline all-street accuracy is misleading. The same baseline model that reports ~5.5 MAE across all streets scores 30.7 on the streets with ground truth — a 5.5× gap. Accuracy must be reported disaggregated (sensored vs imputed).
  • Restricting evaluation/loss to the real streets matters. Both A and B (~22) beat the all-street baseline (~30.7) on the sensor streets — i.e. honest, masked treatment of the target gives a truer and better sensor-street number.
  • Deleting the in-between streets fragments the network. In Exp A's subgraph, 35 of the 74 sensor nodes lost all neighbours, because their neighbours were the unsensored streets that had been removed. This is direct evidence that the unsensored streets are needed as connective tissue, even if not as prediction targets.

Honest limits of the ablation

The Phase-1 numbers are directional, not definitive: a single random seed; Exp A and Exp B used different normalisation statistics (a confound); and Exp B was under-trained (it peaked far earlier than the others). So A (22.34) versus B (22.84) is well within noise and should not be read as "subgraph beats full graph." The robust takeaways are only the three bullets above.

3. Phase 2 — What we changed (data-foundation fixes)

A separate stress-test of the pipeline found five foundation-level issues in how the raw data was prepared. These were corrected (full detail in the companion change report and docs/notes/decisions.md, entries D-011 to D-015):

FixBefore → After
Weather timezoneWeather merged 11 h out of sync → fetched in Melbourne local time (peak temperature moved from a 4 am label to 2 pm)
Foot-traffic scalingOne global scale (busy streets at 4–10 std-devs) → log1p compression before scaling (range now −1.7 to 3.4)
Sensor outagesFilled with zero (looked like quiet streets) → short gaps interpolated, long outages flagged and excluded from training/scoring (6.3% of sensor bins)
Imputed-street confidenceOne blanket value → borrowed from the most similar measured streets
Non-sensor parkingSet to 0% ("empty") → set to the sensor mean ("unknown")

Why the improvement is genuine — and conservative

  • Two fixes pull opposite ways on the score, and the good one won. Excluding the fake-zero outage bins removes easy correct answers (a headwind that should raise error), yet error still fell ~20%. The log1p fix improved numerical conditioning so busy streets are no longer in an unstable range — that gain outweighed the headwind. So the true modelling improvement is larger than 19.5%.
  • R² rose as well as MAE. R² is scale-relative and far less sensitive to the changed evaluation denominator, so its rise (0.890 → 0.911) corroborates a real gain rather than a metric artefact.
  • Parking unchanged, confirming the parking pathway was not disturbed by the changes; training converged cleanly with no instability.

Do not cross-compare Phase 1 and Phase 3 numbers

Phase 3's 4.45 is an average over all 1,397 streets (mostly easy imputed ones); Phase 1's 22–30 are averages over the 74 sensor streets only. They measure different populations and are not comparable — "22.8 → 4.45" would be a false claim. The legitimate comparison is Phase 3 against the previous all-street model (5.54 → 4.45), shown above. The retrained model's sensor-only accuracy (the figure directly comparable to Exp A/B) has not yet been measured and remains to be re-run on the disaggregated test set.

5. What this means, and what is still open

  • Established: evaluation must be disaggregated; the unsensored streets are required for network connectivity (removing them isolates 35 of 74 sensor nodes); the foundation fixes improved the all-street model materially (−19.5% MAE, +0.02 R²) over the previous version.
  • Not yet established: whether the full imputed network improves accuracy on the sensored streets. The current ablation is too weak (single seed, normalisation confound, under-trained Exp B) to decide this in either direction.
  • Next steps to make a rigorous comparative claim: (a) re-measure the retrained model's sensor-only MAE so it is comparable to Exp A/B; (b) run a hardened ablation — three conditions (sensor-only subgraph; full graph + masked loss; full graph + all-street loss), identical normalisation, identical epoch budget, ≥5 seeds, reported as mean ± std.
