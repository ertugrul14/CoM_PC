# NYC DOT (2023) — Curb Management Action Plan

**Full citation:** New York City Department of Transportation. (2023). *Curb Management Action Plan*.
NYC DOT. https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/about/curb-management.shtml

## What they did
- Policy document outlining 10 action items for NYC curb lane reform
- Defines 5 curb function categories: Transportation Access, Public Realm,
  Services & Safety, Vehicle Storage, Circulation & Movement
- Introduces Smart Curbs pilot concept (BID-level blank-slate curb reprogramming)
- Demand-based parking pricing proof-of-concept
- PUDO zone designation for FHVs

## Key findings / policy positions
- Only 80,000 of ~3 million NYC curbside spaces are currently metered
- Median income of car owners ($90,100) is ~2× non-car owners ($45,769) citywide
- 40% pre-COVID deliveries were residential → 80% post-COVID
- Protected bike lanes show 34% reduction in total injuries on corridors
- Curb hierarchy: Transit/loading > Public realm > Bike > Parking (context-dependent)
- Flexibility principle: same block can change function by time-of-day and day-of-week

## How this supports the thesis
- Policy framing for scenario simulation: flex-use curb = real-world precedent
- Justifies using parking occupancy reduction as proxy for curb repurposing
- "Flexibility windows" concept in our Step 11 directly maps to their time-of-day/day-of-week
  flex programming principle
- Equity argument: parking benefits minority (car owners, higher income) → repurposing
  benefits majority (pedestrians, cyclists, transit users) → justifies pedestrian-first scenarios
