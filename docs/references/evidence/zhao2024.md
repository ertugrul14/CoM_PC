# Zhao & Zhang (2024) — AGCRU Adaptive GCN+GRU, Melbourne

**Full citation:** Zhao, X., & Zhang, M. (2024). Enhancing predictive models for on-street parking
occupancy: Integrating Adaptive GCN and GRU with household categories and POI factors.
*Mathematics*, 12(18), 2823.

## What they did
- AGCRU model: Adaptive GCN (learnable adjacency matrix) + GRU
- Features: parking sensors + POI distribution + residential housing category
- Same dataset: Melbourne CBD on-street parking sensors
- Validated at 15-min, 30-min, 60-min prediction horizons

## Key findings
- Adaptive (learnable) adjacency matrix outperforms static k-NN graph
- MAE: 0.0156 (15-min), 0.0330 (30-min), 0.0558 (60-min)
- RMSE: 0.0244, 0.0665, 0.1003 for same intervals
- MAPE: 1.56%, 3.31%, 5.58%
- POI and household type integration improves accuracy beyond temporal-only models
- Outperforms ARIMA, LSTM, ST-GCN, ASTGCN, HST-GCN baselines

## How this supports the thesis
- **Same city (Melbourne)** — directly comparable baseline numbers
- MAPE < 2% at 15-min is the benchmark to beat or match for parking prediction
- Adaptive adjacency justification: our mutual k-NN semantic graph is a principled alternative
- Baseline comparison set (ARIMA, LSTM, ST-GCN, ASTGCN, HST-GCN) adopted for our evaluation
- POI feature importance: confirms café count, bar capacity, business count as key features

## Key numbers to cite (as benchmarks)
- MAE 0.0156, RMSE 0.0244, MAPE 1.56% at 15-min (parking, Melbourne)
- These are the numbers our model needs to beat or be close to

## Limitations they note
- Only parking prediction (no pedestrian co-modelling)
- Static study period, no seasonal variation analysis
